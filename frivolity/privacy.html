Privacy Policy

Frivolity · last updated 1 November 2026

Frivolity is a puzzle game. There is no Frivolity account and nothing you type. The only sign-in is Apple's own Game Center, and only if you turn on the leaderboards. This policy explains the little data that does exist and who touches it.

What stays on your device

Your scores, your records and the game in progress are stored on your iPhone. If you are signed in to iCloud, your records are also saved to your own Apple Account so a new iPhone finds them again. That copy lives in your iCloud account; it is not sent to the developer and the developer cannot read it. Deleting the app removes the local copy; removing the app's iCloud data is done from your device's iCloud settings.

What the developer collects

Nothing. There is no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting, and no server of the developer's that Frivolity talks to. No personal information ever reaches the developer.

Game Center leaderboards

Leaderboards are off unless you turn them on, from the bouquet shelf. Until you do, Frivolity never signs in to Game Center and no score leaves your iPhone.

If you turn them on, Frivolity sends your scores to Apple's Game Center: your best score so far, and the score of each game you finish. They appear on the leaderboards under your Game Center name, where other Game Center players can see them. Game Center is run by Apple under your Apple Account, and Apple describes what it collects in Game Center & Privacy. Frivolity keeps a note on your iPhone of the highest score Game Center has confirmed, so it is not sent twice.

Turning the switch off stops any further scores being sent. Scores already posted stay on the leaderboards; you can manage your Game Center profile and data in Settings › Game Center.

Advertising

Frivolity is free and shows ads supplied by Google AdMob. To do that, Google's advertising SDK may collect and process device information such as an advertising identifier, coarse location derived from IP address, and data about how ads were shown and interacted with. This is done by Google, not by the developer, and the developer does not receive that data.

Google's handling of it is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. You can limit ad personalisation on your iPhone under Settings › Privacy & Security › Apple Advertising, and in the tracking permission Frivolity asks for if it asks at all.

Where the law requires it — in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom — Frivolity asks for your consent before personalised ads are shown, and your choice is respected for as long as it is stored on your device. You can change or withdraw that consent at any time from Ad privacy choices, at the foot of the field guide.

On iPhone, Frivolity also asks Apple's own tracking permission before any advertising identifier is used to personalise ads across apps. Declining changes nothing about the game: you get every feature, and less relevant ads. You can change the answer later under Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.

Children

Frivolity is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

How long data is kept

Data on your device is kept until you delete the app or clear it. Records in iCloud are kept until you remove them from your Apple Account. Scores posted to Game Center are held by Apple under its own policy, linked above. Data held by Google is subject to Google's own retention policy, linked above.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date.
